# Task Instructions
Determine whether an informal mathematical theorem statement (in natural language) and a formal mathematical statement (written in Lean 4) define **exactly the same concept**. Your goal is to judge if the two are semantically equivalent in all respects, meaning they define precisely the same theorem.

Important constraints:
- Mathlib (Lean's mathematics library) is treated as valid mathematical context—that is, you may use it as a background/reference to check meaning.
- **However, it is not acceptable for a model to simply output the name of a theorem from Mathlib as a means of aligning the two.** You must compare the full content/meaning of the two statements, not just their names or aliases.
- Do **not** align statements if they differ even slightly in subtle details, implicit assumptions, quantifiers, or the scope of what is being concluded.
- Err on the side of caution—if there is any ambiguity or mismatch, output "misaligned".
- If either of the informal or formal statements are corrupted in any way (i.e., the informal statement is gibberish, the formal statement isn't valid Lean 4 code, etc.), output "misaligned".

Process details:
- Carefully analyze and compare the *full logical and semantic content* of both definitions before making any judgment.
- Consider all relevant mathematical subtleties, implicit context, quantifiers, and definitional precision.
- If the formal definition is simply a re-naming or direct call to a Mathlib definition (e.g., `def foo := Nat.prime`), classify this as "misaligned" unless the full statement is restated and matches completely in content.

Reasoning and Output Order:
- First, provide a thorough reasoning/explanation *before* presenting your final classification (alignment judgment). Reasoning must address any nuances, differences, or complete equivalence.
- Second, output the final classification: "aligned" or "misaligned", and nothing more.

# Output Format

Always output a single JSON object with two fields:
- "reasoning": (string; your explanation/justification, always appearing first)
- "result": (string; either "aligned" or "misaligned", always appearing last)

Example:
{
  "reasoning": "[detailed explanation of comparison, differences, and rationale for your classification]",
  "result": "[either 'aligned' or 'misaligned']"
}

open Filter Topology
theorem seq_tendsto_iff_subseq
  (u : ℕ → ℝ) (l : ℝ) :
  Tendsto u atTop (𝓝 l) ↔
  ∀ φ : ℕ → ℕ, StrictMono φ → Tendsto (fun n => u (φ n)) atTop (𝓝 l) := by sorry
